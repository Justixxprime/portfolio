ShelemJ
Resources

A 20 page site for a real African food export company, built to search engines' real specifications rather than guessed at.

LAGOS, NIGERIA EXPORTED WORLDWIDE EST. 2013
justixxprime.github.io/shelemj-website

The client

ShelemJ Resources Limited exports African food products. The site was built as a plain HTML project, styled with Tailwind through its CLI build rather than a framework, across 20 pages.

A real rebrand history, not invented backstory

The About page tells the company's actual history rather than a generic "who we are": founded in 2013 as Jueco Foods, exporting African made food and raw materials to European markets including the Netherlands and Belgium, rebranded in 2021 as Emma Worldwide, then restructured again to become ShelemJ. The homepage stats bar reflects that same history: four product categories, over 55 export markets, and more than five years exporting under the current name.

A real product catalog, not a single page

Products are organized into six categories, Spices and Seasonings, African Made Beverages, Grains and Raw Materials, Premium Oils, Dried Seeds and Vegetables, plus an All view, each filterable on the Products page. A downloadable PDF of the full product catalog sits alongside it for a buyer who wants something to forward internally rather than a link.

Real structured data, not guesswork

Every one of the 20 pages carries JSON LD structured data, Organization and FAQPage schema, added after correcting a prior audit that had gotten the details wrong. This is what lets a business's own facts, its name, its FAQ answers, surface directly inside Google search results rather than staying invisible to it.

The logo decision

An early draft of the site had a Trusted By section with client logos meant to signal credibility. Those logo files did not actually exist. Rather than leave a placeholder or invent something that looked plausible, that section was replaced with badges the business could genuinely verify: CAC registration, NEPC registration, and its established year. It is a smaller, less flashy claim, but every part of it is true.

A bug solved twice

The same backdrop filter issue that broke Amani Community Trust's mobile menu also showed up here, and was fixed the same way, by understanding the actual mechanism rather than trying different CSS until something happened to work.

What I would change today: the FAQ content was written before the structured data was added. If I rebuilt this now, I would write the FAQ answers with the schema in mind from the start, since a couple of them are longer than what displays well in a search result snippet.

WHY TAILWIND CLI, NOT A FRAMEWORK
Requirement20 pages of mostly static content, product listings, and a blog, with no interactive application logic
Constrainta React or Vite setup adds a build step and complexity that this content does not need
Decisionplain HTML compiled with the Tailwind CLI, no JavaScript framework at all
Consequencesimpler deployment and a smaller page weight, at the cost of manually repeating shared markup like the header and footer across every page

A framework would have made shared components easier to manage across 20 pages. For a site with no client side application logic, that convenience did not outweigh the added build complexity for a solo developer to maintain.
